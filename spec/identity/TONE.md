# TONE.md - Reway

> This is the machine-readable brand voice contract for writers, agents, and downstream content skills. Preserve its section order when updating it.

## Context

- **Brand:** Reway
- **Category:** B2C/D2C personal bookmark manager.
- **Mission (1 line):** Make saving links instant and make them easy to find again.
- **Primary audience:** Researchers, developers, designers, heavy tab users, and people who need a calmer alternative to browser-native bookmarks.
- **Secondary audiences:** Existing users managing large personal libraries and people preserving browser sessions.
- **Market:** Global.
- **Language:** English only. Use en-US spelling and mechanics.
- **Reading-age target:** Adult general. Technical detail belongs only on explicitly technical surfaces.
- **Channels in scope:** Public website, dashboard UI, Chrome extension UI, and email.
- **Primary content goal:** Help people understand that Reway saves links now and makes them easier to retrieve later.
- **Risk tolerance:** Moderately distinctive. Calm, precise, personal, and restrained rather than playful, corporate, or aspirational.
- **Regulatory constraints:** Do not make unsupported privacy, security, performance, sync, AI, or availability claims.

## Voice Attributes

### 1. Precise, never verbose

**Definition:** Every word earns its place. Reway says the thing, then stops.

**Sounds like:** "Save a tab. It's in your library."

**Does not sound like:** "With Reway, you can easily save your currently open browser tab directly to your personal bookmark library."

**Do:**

- Lead with the outcome, not the feature explanation.
- Use short sentences to land key points.
- Trust the reader to connect the dots.

**Don't:**

- Use "in order to" when "to" works.
- Repeat what the preceding sentence established.
- Say something "allows you to" do a thing. Say what Reway does.

### 2. Calm, never hype

**Definition:** Reway is confident without exclamation marks, superlatives, or launch-day energy for normal actions.

**Sounds like:** "All your links. One place."

**Does not sound like:** "A powerful, game-changing bookmark manager that will revolutionize your workflow."

**Do:**

- Make declarative statements.
- Write as if the reader already understands why saving matters.
- Let restraint signal confidence.

**Don't:**

- Use generic modifiers such as "powerful," "seamless," or "next-level."
- Use hype to describe ordinary product actions.
- Make background enrichment feel urgent.

### 3. Honest, never salesy

**Definition:** Say what the product does, including its timing and limits. Specific or silent.

**Sounds like:** "Your bookmark saves now. Its title and favicon follow."

**Does not sound like:** "Experience the transformative power of intelligent bookmark management."

**Do:**

- Name the actual benefit.
- Describe asynchronous enrichment plainly when it matters.
- Prefer "bookmark" and "library" to broad metaphors.

**Don't:**

- Promise perfect organization, recall, or automation.
- Hide a limitation behind vague language.
- Call metadata enrichment AI in user-facing copy.

### 4. Personal, never corporate

**Definition:** Reway speaks to one person and their private library. It is not a team workspace or a business platform.

**Sounds like:** "Your links. Your order. Your way."

**Does not sound like:** "Reway provides users with a comprehensive bookmark management solution."

**Do:**

- Use "you" and "your" freely.
- Treat the library as private and personal.
- Use active voice where it stays clear.

**Don't:**

- Call people "users" or "customers" in user-facing copy.
- Use B2B language such as "stakeholders," "leverage," or "solution."
- Imply sharing, collaboration, or public discovery where it does not exist.

### 5. Confident, never arrogant

**Definition:** Reway has clear opinions about capture and retrieval, but never needs to attack browser bookmarks or competitors.

**Sounds like:** "Save first. Sort later. Or never. That works too."

**Does not sound like:** "Unlike outdated bookmark managers, Reway fixes your broken workflow."

**Do:**

- State product opinions directly.
- Make failure states plain and non-alarming.
- Show confidence through the product's clear boundaries.

**Don't:**

- Compare against competitors by name.
- Use defensive framing.
- Use false modesty or product-category put-downs.

## Archetype

- **Rationale:** Reway is a craft tool for people who curate their own thinking and references. The Creator shapes a personal library; the Sage makes retrieval feel guided and frictionless. Reway is not Explorer-led: it keeps what people have already found.

### Banned terms

leverage, utilize, streamline, optimize, empower, unlock, transform, revolutionize, game-changing, next-level, seamless, robust, comprehensive, solution, platform, stakeholders, "allows you to", exciting, vibrant, groundbreaking, innovative, delve, tapestry, and landscape when used abstractly.

### Power words

Use intentionally, not decoratively: instant, calm, yours, clear, fast, organized, precise, personal, found, saved, ready.

### Jargon policy

- Public copy and product UI use product language, not implementation language.
- Explain a technical mechanism only when it helps a person decide what happened or what to do.
- Terms such as SSRF, RLS, Edge Function, Realtime, fractional indexing, and Manifest V3 belong in technical documentation, not user-facing product copy.

### Naming conventions

- Brand: **Reway** (capital R).
- Product surfaces: dashboard, extension, library, canvas.
- Features: bookmark, collection, tag, command search, quick save, session save.
- Use sentence case. Avoid competitor references in user-facing copy.

## Inclusive Language

- Use gender-neutral language by default.
- Avoid ability metaphors such as "blind to" or "falls on deaf ears."
- Do not assume age, geography, device skill, or internet access.
- Avoid "easy," "simple," and "just" in UI copy.

## Channel-Specific Guidance

### Website

The fullest expression of Reway's voice: precise, calm, and personal. Headlines are declarative, not questions. Benefits come before features. The site should feel like the product: organized, quietly confident, and clear.

- Headlines have a maximum of 6 words.
- Do not use rhetorical hero questions.
- Do not lead with technical stack, AI, or implementation detail.
- Replace "Get started today" with the actual first action.

### In-product UI

The tersest channel. Actions are verbs. States are short phrases. Errors say what happened and what to do next. Toasts are only for user-initiated confirmations, never background enrichment states.

- Labels are verb-first: "Save tab," "Add to collection," "Open in canvas."
- Errors follow: "[What happened]. [What to do]." Use no more than 2 sentences.
- Empty states explain what belongs here and how to begin.
- Enrichment failure appears on the bookmark card, not in a toast.
- Direct-paste capture guidance belongs only in the command surface footer, not as permanent dashboard copy.

### Extension UI

Space is constrained. Labels must make sense without surrounding copy. The extension extends the dashboard; it does not create a separate personality.

- Use 1–3-word labels where possible.
- Prefer "Save tab" to "Save this tab to your Reway library."
- Avoid explanatory copy inside the popup unless it prevents a destructive or irreversible action.

### Email

Slightly warmer than product UI, still precise. Transactional email remains informational. Announcements explain what is new, why it matters, and the single next action.

- Subject lines are sentence case, under 50 characters, with no exclamation marks.
- Greet with "Hi [name]" and sign off "Reway."
- Use one verb-first CTA per email.

## Global Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Lead with the outcome.
- Use "you" and "your" for the personal library.
- Keep key moments short.
- State what something does, not what it enables.
- Be honest about asynchronous behaviour and limitations.
- Use sentence case and active voice.
- Keep technical stack details as secondary proof.

### Don't

- Use banned words, generic superlatives, or empty productivity claims.
- Use exclamation marks for ordinary product events.
- Lead marketing copy with the stack or an AI claim.
- Describe Reway as a workspace, knowledge base, collaboration tool, or platform.
- Mention competitors by name or make the category sound broken.
- Over-explain, use passive filler, or stack buzzwords.
